How do they work?

Straight Scissors: These are the most common type of pet grooming scissors, used for general trimming of the pet's fur. They can be used to trim fur on the pet's body, face, ears, and tail.

Thinning Scissors: These scissors have one straight-toothed side and one serrated side, typically used for adjusting and thinning out the pet's fur to make it look more natural and smooth. They are ideal for handling excessively dense or long hair and can prevent the hair from knotting.

Up-Curved Scissors: These scissors have a curved blade, used for trimming the fur on the pet's face, ears, paws, and tail. Because of the curved design of the blades, they can easily reach areas where straight scissors cannot, such as the fur around the eyes or inside the ears.

Down-Curved Scissors: These scissors have a blade that curves downward, used for trimming certain parts of the pet's body, like the chest and belly, to prevent cutting the pet.

Chunkers: This is a special type of grooming scissors, which typically have wider teeth and can remove large amounts of hair without leaving obvious cutting lines. These scissors are particularly useful when trimming thick or long-haired pets and are often used to create soft, natural transitions, such as between the body and limbs, or the head and neck.

The above text is for reference only, more usage methods can be determined based on the groomer's experience. It is important to always prioritize the comfort and safety of the pet when using grooming scissors. When cutting fur, try to avoid contact with the pet's skin as much as possible.
